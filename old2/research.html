Research

Repeats & transposable elements · Published review

How mobile DNA helps insects adapt

Transposable elements can move to new places in a genome. In our review, Cheng Sun and I examined cases where this activity changes a trait that matters to the insect, from surviving an insecticide to developing a different wing pattern.

We traced four routes by which active elements contribute to adaptation. They can alter gene expression, rearrange chromosomes, change epigenetic regulation or move between species. The figures below connect these mechanisms to examples from insects.

Repeats & transposable elements · Master’s thesis in progress

Transposon-derived genes in the bumblebee brain

Some former transposable elements have persisted across bumblebee species. We found that several of these genes are expressed in particular brain cell types. I’m investigating whether they have taken on regulatory roles in the nervous system.

We combined genome annotation with single-nucleus, bulk and long-read transcriptomics to characterize the candidates. Conservation across species and evidence of purifying selection suggest that some have retained a function. Changes in gene expression after knockdown point to possible downstream targets.

Bioinformatics platforms · Live database

iBumblebee

Bumblebee omics data are distributed across many individual studies. We built iBumblebee so researchers can browse genome assemblies, explore gene expression and compare species within a single resource.

The platform brings together genomic and transcriptomic datasets, interactive tools and precomputed evolutionary analyses. I’m maintaining the database and planning new taxonomy and single-cell modules. The associated manuscript is under revision at Molecular Ecology Resources.

Mathematical modeling · Machine learning · AI for Science

Models for species and genes

My background in programming, machine learning and mathematical modeling at Chongqing University of Posts and Telecommunications shaped how I approach biology. I’m especially interested in models that help us interpret the biology behind a prediction.

BuzzID

BuzzID identifies bumblebee species from a DNA barcode, with the option to include a specimen photograph. The model supports 109 Bombus species and is also available through iBumblebee.
